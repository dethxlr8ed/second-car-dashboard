    Running costs

    Yearly cost = fuel + insurance (estimate) + tires + maintenance + registration, at 10,000 miles a year. The bar on each card splits it: Fuel Insurance (est.) Tires Maintenance Registration and smog. Bars share one scale, so a longer bar costs more per year.

    3-year total = price + 10.25% Fremont sales tax + DMV transfer and registration + $85 doc fee + $225 pre-purchase inspection + any immediate check (for example a Prius battery scan) + three years of running costs. 4-year total uses the same upfront amount plus four years of running costs (registration counted once in the first year). Resale value is not subtracted. For LA-region cars, the card also shows one-way fuel to drive that car home, at its EPA combined MPG and AAA Oakland gas prices, plus a sourced estimate for a one-way flight or Amtrak ticket so a buyer can get there and drive back. Insurance is an estimate (California minimum coverage plus average comprehensive, scaled by model) and will vary by driver. Fuel uses AAA Oakland prices for Nov 12-13, 2026: $5.62 regular, $6.10 premium. Tire sets are Tire Rack prices for a budget and a premium all-season, before installation; the yearly tire cost spreads a budget set plus install over its mileage warranty. The five manual cars in the original followed set were not costed and show a dash. Search-batch fuel uses EPA combined MPG from fueleconomy.gov; private sales carry no dealer doc fee. Hybrid listings (Prius and Camry Hybrid) also show a 3-year battery-replacement reserve next to the base 3-year total; the reserve is not added into the ranked cost used for sorting.

    Price sources: CarCareBase (ranges checked Nov 9, 2026, covering 2004-2015 Prius and Camry Hybrid). GreenTec current pack prices, fetched Nov 14, 2026: Prius 2004-2009 and 2010-2015 reman or high-capacity cells $1,499, new cells $2,349 (GreenTec Prius); 2007-2011 Camry Hybrid reman $1,699, new cells $2,449 (GreenTec Camry reman, new cells). Install is typically $150-$250 (Hybrid Battery Swap). The $1,700 / $1,750 planning numbers are reman pack plus install, which is the path a sub-$10k buyer would usually take. A May 2024 PriusChat quote put a BumbleBee new pack at $2,250 plus $150 to install.

    Lifespan sources: ALTEGA (Sacramento shop ranges: Gen 2 10-15 years / 150k-250k miles, Gen 3 8-12 years / 120k-200k, Camry Hybrid 2007-2017 10-14 years / 150k-220k); Atomic Auto (most original Prius packs 150k-200k miles in a mild climate, some past 250k). Heat and a blocked battery cooling fan shorten life. Age matters as well as miles, so a low-mileage 18-year-old pack can still be tired.

    Warranty. None of these 2005-2013 listings is still covered. Pre-2020 Toyota hybrids had 8 years or 100,000 miles of hybrid-battery coverage nationwide. California-spec 2004-2017 Prius and 2007-2017 Camry Hybrid also had a 10 year or 150,000 mile term under the California emissions warranty, in California and some other states (Toyota T-SB-0074-20, summarized at hybridbatterytest.com). Toyota later made 10 years or 150,000 miles the nationwide battery term for 2020 and newer hybrids (Toyota). A 2013 is 13 years old in 2026, so even the California term is expired. The 2008 Prius in San Diego is under 150,000 miles but is 18 years old, so it is expired too.

    Health indicators. Before buying, run a Dr. Prius / Dr. Hybrid OBD test (block voltages, and a paid capacity or life-expectancy test) or a shop scan ($100-$200; Prius upfront costs on this dashboard already include a $135 Hybrid Battery Solutions deep scan). Warning signs: falling mpg, a battery gauge that swings quickly, the engine running more often, a red triangle or Check Hybrid System light, or reduced power. A weak 12-volt battery can look like a dead hybrid pack (Atomic Auto, CarCareBase).

    Gen 2 and Gen 3 issues, besides the pack. The inverter coolant pump is a wear item on 2004-2015 Prius. Specialists typically charge $300-$600 installed (Belyer, $300 for 2004-2015; Tucson Hybrids, $300-$600; Car Fault Finder, $150-$400). If the pump is ignored, inverter damage can run $2,500-$5,000. Gen 3 (2010-2015) also has EGR cooler and intake clogging, and head-gasket failures that are cited most often on 2010-2011 cars: EGR service about $250-$800 (Ask CarFix, Car Fault Finder, October 2026); head-gasket repair about $1,500-$3,500 at an independent hybrid shop, or $2,880-$3,974 on RepairPal for a 2010. The 2007-2011 Camry Hybrid used the 2AZ-FE engine in Toyota's ZE7 oil-consumption program; that campaign is expired, so check oil use at a PPI.

    How the reserve is calculated. Risk assumes the original pack is still in the car (service records here are not complete enough to confirm a prior replacement). High: already at or past typical upper life in miles, or past typical upper age and past typical lower miles (75% over 3 years). Med: already at or past the typical lower life in age or miles, but not High (50%). Low: still below the lower bound on both even after 3 years and 30,000 miles (20%). Reserve = that probability times the aftermarket reman installed planning cost.
